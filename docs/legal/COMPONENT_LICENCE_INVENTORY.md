# Component-to-licence inventory

Generated by `scripts/build_component_licence_inventory.py` from the table in that script. Do not edit by hand; `tests/test_component_licence_inventory.py` regenerates it and fails if this file differs.

This inventory states the **intended category** of each part of the repository, from the owner's licensing decision of 2026-11-10, and flags what cannot be covered automatically. It is not legal advice, it decides no legal question, and nothing in it says that any component or distribution is cleared. The unresolved question whether the Commons Clause can stand beside AGPL-3.0 (sections 7 and 10) is for counsel (D-LIC-3); this document does not answer it.

Categories: **1 Core** = GNU AGPL-3.0-or-later with the Commons Clause (source-available, not OSI open source). **2 Apps** = GNU AGPL-3.0-only, without the Commons Clause. **3 Third-party** = the third party's own licence, unchanged. **-** = not a licensed component.

## 3. Flags: what cannot be covered automatically

**F1. Authorship by an AI agent.** Four commits of 2026-11-04 (the local-capability gate for the loopback API and `docs/LOCAL_API_SECURITY.md`) have the author `Claude (security agent) <noreply@anthropic.com>`. `LICENSE.md` ("Contribution history") states that all authoring commits are by RedevOps. Whether code produced with an AI agent under the owner's direction is covered by the owner's copyright, and whether that sentence stays accurate as written, is for counsel (N4). Not decided here.

**F2. Identity mapping of authors.** Commits carry three author identities of the owner (two Alex Mats addresses and the `arybach` GitHub account, mostly through the merge button). `LICENSE.md` says these are one rightsholder; that mapping is the owner's statement and is not independently evidenced in the repository. No outside contribution has been merged (see `CONTRIBUTING.md`). Counsel question N4.

**F3. Third-party text under its own licence.** These paths hold other parties' texts or list other parties' packages. ReDevOps licensing does not apply to them and cannot be applied to them; they are collected, not relicensed. Their notices are preserved in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`.

**F4. Separate repository, licence statement not re-read here.** The Projects UI bundle is built from the separate `redevops-projects` repository. The pinned source commit is a local, unpublished commit (`packaging/ui-bundle-source.json`), and the repository's own licence statement was not re-read for this inventory (D-LIC-2). The core statement lists the Projects UI as a core component on the owner's decision; the bundle also contains React, ReactDOM and scheduler (MIT), which stay MIT.

**F5. Applications wrap third-party cores.** Several applications are an agent layer over a third-party self-hosted core (for example Lago, Chatwoot, Umami, Listmonk, changedetection.io, Metabase). The app code is category 2; the wrapped core, its images and its data stay under the core's own licence and are not relicensed by anything in this repository.

**F6. Apps import the Commons-Clause core.** About 190 files under `apps/` import `agentic_os`. Whether AGPL-3.0-only applications may depend on a Commons-Clause core as intended, and whether an AGPL section 7 additional permission is needed, is counsel question N1 / D-LIC-3. This inventory states the intended category only.

**F7. Third-party dependency of an integration.** `integrations/voice-pipecat` depends on `pipecat-ai` (third-party, BSD-2-Clause as published) which keeps its own licence. The integration's package metadata uses a free-text licence string, not the `LicenseRef-` identifier of the core.

**F8. Authorship of images and icons not recorded.** The repository images, launcher icons and the `apps/social-autopilot/reel/assets/hero.jpg` file have no authorship record in the repository. D-LIC-8 and D-LIC-11 ask the owner to confirm first-party authorship; until confirmed they cannot be covered automatically.

**F9. Apps that carry a third-party-derived name or assets.** An app directory may hold data files, samples or text taken from a third-party project. None was found by the mechanical scan (no vendored directories, no minified third-party scripts, no copyright notice of another party in tracked source), but the scan is not an audit; a licence scan of each app is part of the counsel review (N1).

## 4. What this inventory does not do

* It does not relicense, retag or reclassify any third-party or mixed-ownership material.
* It does not add per-file SPDX headers; the app-level `LICENSE` files and the statements in `LICENSING.md` carry the terms for this release.
* It does not settle the AGPL section 7 / section 10 and Commons Clause question (D-LIC-3), the relationship of the AGPL-3.0-only apps to the core they import (N1), the scope of "Software" in the Commons Clause (N2), the contributor mechanism (N3) or the copyright evidence (N4).
